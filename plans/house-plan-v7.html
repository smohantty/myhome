THE IDEA

  • One bold gable. A 38° standing-seam roof with its ridge running north–south, so the gable end faces the road and the mountains. Black timber outside, oak lining inside, white render on the wing: the classic Nordic dark-house / white-box pair.
  • A glass gable over a double-height great room. From the floor to the ridge, 20½′ wide. Sit by the stove and the mountains fill the wall; the gallery and the master both look out through the same glass.
  • Floating oak stair in one straight run up the blank east wall (17 risers of 7″), behind a full-height oak slat screen that doubles as the gallery balustrade.
  • Calm plan: a spine wall splits the view side (great room, snug) from the working side (kitchen, dining, library). Upstairs every room is vaulted to the roof.
  • The wing is the spa: boot room and side door, guest suite, cedar sauna; above it a bedroom that opens onto a hot-tub deck under a pergola.

HONEST TRADE-OFFS

  • Vastu set aside, as briefed: the door is in the south-east half, the stair on the east, the sauna and baths in the north-east. V4–V6 remain the Vastu options.
  • South glass + Indian sun: use low-e double glazing; the 1½′ roof overhang and the birches shade it, and add external screens or blinds for May–June afternoons.
  • The void costs ≈ 305 sq ft of first-floor area, which is why there are 3 bedrooms upstairs, not 4.
  • Ridge ≈ 32½′: check the local height limit for G+1. The roof void above the eaves is not usable floor.
  • The wood stove can be a gas or bio-ethanol stove with the same black flue if solid fuel isn't wanted.

Concept plan. Have a local architect check structure, bye-law setbacks, heights and stair headroom before drawings.
